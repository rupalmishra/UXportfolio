Calmory · Diet Planner 2026

A diet planner that knows what people eat in your region

Calmory generates meal plans built around a person's actual region, grocery budget, cooking time, and fasting practices — then tracks what they actually ate against it.

The MVP
01

Region-locked plans

A pincode sets the plan to what is actually eaten and sold there. No foreign ingredients, no imported diet advice.

02

Fasting mode

Fasting days are part of the plan, not a break from it. The day rebuilds itself around what the person can eat.

03

Adapts to the real day

Log what was actually eaten and the rest of the plan adjusts, instead of marking the day a failure.

01 · Problem statement

Regional food is missing.

Meal plans skew Western or generic North Indian, so daily suggestions feel irrelevant and get ignored.

Fasting breaks tracking.

On vrat or auspicious days the current apps have nothing useful to say, so the streak and the habit both break.

Grocery is a separate chore.

You get a plan in one place and then have to manually figure out what to buy, which kills follow through.

02 · Project goals

1

Make regional food the default, not an afterthought.

2

Treat fasting as a normal part of the week.

3

Make daily logging feel effortless.

4

Close the gap between planning and shopping.

04 · Design exploration

Four iterations of the home screen

Paper first. From stacked metrics to a Today/Week split, separate protein and calorie rings, and clearer meal categories.

v1

Arc meters + weekday strip

Protein and calories as arcs, with the week running across the top.

v2

Today/Week tabs, one combined ring

Split the timeframe, but a single ring hid which target was missed.

v3

Separate P & C rings, Add-to-Meal

Two rings read faster, and logging moved onto the card itself.

v4

Meal categories B/L/S/D

Breakfast, lunch, snacks and dinner became the spine of the screen.

05 · Final designs

Onboarding

Four steps collect what the plan actually needs: pincode for regional cuisine, goal and activity, allergies and cooking time, and the days non-veg is on the table.

Home and fasting

One toggle turns the day into a fasting day. Grains, onion and garlic drop out of the picks and the plan re-curates in place.

Browsing meals

Category screens are filtered to what people actually cook in that region, with calories and protein visible before you commit.

Logging what you ate

Photo, search or plain language. If the day went sideways, the app estimates it and rebalances the rest instead of breaking the streak.

Grocery

The week’s plan becomes a priced list, grouped by aisle, ready to order in one tap.

07 · Future scope

→Grocery ordering integration: Calmory generates the list, let users order it in one tap via a delivery partner.
→Community and sharing: Let users share meal plans and rate dishes, building the habit socially.
→Wearable and calendar sync: Auto-adjust targets from step/workout data, and pre-set fasting days from the calendar.
→Restaurant menu suggestions: Recommend dishes at nearby restaurants that fit your macro targets.

08 · Learnings

Simplicity drives consistency — fewer taps to log a meal means users are more likely to build a daily habit.
Data visualization builds motivation — presenting macros through charts and progress rings makes goals feel tangible and trackable.
